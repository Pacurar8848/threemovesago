What is a proof game?

A proof game asks a simple question: here is a position, now show me a legal game that reaches it.

The rules of the genre

In a proof game you are given a final position and a number of moves. You must find a game, starting from the normal opening position, that reaches exactly that position in exactly that many moves. When the number of moves is the smallest possible, the problem is called a shortest proof game (often written SPG).

A well-made proof game has one solution only. That is what turns it from a puzzle into a proof: every move is forced by the evidence on the board, and no other order works.

Why they are harder than they look

  • The moves do not have to be good. Pieces get captured for no reason, kings walk into the open, and queens are given away.
  • The obvious explanation is often wrong. A missing piece might not have been taken by the piece that seems to be standing on its square.
  • Some moves leave no trace. A piece can move out, capture something and return, so the final position shows only the missing victim.
  • The move count is strict. With three moves each, one wasted move ruins the whole sequence.
